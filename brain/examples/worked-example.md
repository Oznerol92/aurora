A model article showing the method's techniques in action (mechanics only — the personal story is illustrative, not a fact to claim as Aurora's own):

- **Hook + number opening.** Opens on a concrete spend figure and a contrasting value figure ("I spent a small amount; the equivalent would have cost a large amount"), then a paragraph that immediately starts justifying the gap. No "AI is changing everything" lead.
- **Named problem before solution.** States three concrete failure modes the author fell into, each with its consequence, before offering any fix — earning the solution rather than asserting it.
- **Method in numbered steps.** A small fixed number of steps, each saying what it is, why it matters, and its outcome; operational enough to redo.
- **Transparent math on the value claim.** The headline "worth $X" number is built in the open: a rate range × an hours range, shown as a table of real metrics, landing on a deliberately conservative figure plus a higher realistic one. Round, illustrative figures used to demonstrate the _technique_ of showing your work — never dropped as bare assertions.
- **Explicit "when NOT to use this" section.** A dedicated anti-hype block listing specific scenarios where the method is overkill or a waste, placed after the positive sections as a credibility marker.
- **Documented anti-patterns.** Concrete recurring mistakes, each paired with the consequence of making it.
- **Honest disclaimers in-line.** Self-imposed limits stated plainly ("I don't claim this scales arbitrarily…", "the method only works because you already know the domain well enough to catch when the output is wrong"), rather than papered over.
- **Non-sentimental close.** Hands judgment back to the reader ("Use it. Adapt it.") with a sober pointer to any extended version, framed as a pointer not an ad.

The takeaway: credibility is manufactured by the honesty sections (transparent math, "when not to use", disclaimers, documented mistakes), not by confidence.
