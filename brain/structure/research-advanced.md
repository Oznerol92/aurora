Practices that sit on top of the 6-module research method for multi-session projects.

**Multi-session synthesis & knowledge graph.** After each session extract named entities (orgs, regulations, projects, people, technologies) into one running document mapping their relationships. Before each new session, scan it for entities mentioned but not deeply covered — those are candidate Category-A gaps. After 5+ sessions the graph itself becomes the project's structural backbone. Cross-research synthesis: findings appearing across multiple sessions are stable; findings appearing in only one need verification — they may be a real direction-changer or a one-off confabulation.

**Anti-demotion discipline (the highest-leverage habit).** In the audit phase you will unconsciously demote inconvenient findings to "minor / edge case / not blocking" under an implicit framing of keeping scope manageable. When you catch that label, stop and make the framing explicit, then re-decide. Re-interrogated findings often turn out to be the direction-changers. It will not feel natural; that is why it works.

**Cost optimization.** Run a cheap "list 20-30 macro-areas" discovery session BEFORE the expensive mapping session, so coverage isn't incomplete. Use narrow closing sessions for Category-A gaps rather than broad new ones. Stop running deep sessions on already-mapped territory (watch the marginal direction-changing ratio; below ~5% it's waste). Use lower-tier/lower-effort settings for downstream extraction and synthesis, not the deepest preset.

**Troubleshooting (failure mode → fix):**

- Shallow output → strengthen neutral exploratory framing; split the domain; demand named entities/events, not summaries.
- Vague sources ("reports indicate") → re-state acceptable source types; instruct "if no primary source, omit or label unavailable."
- Covers-but-incomplete → your domain list was short; run a macro-area discovery pass first.
- Real source, wrong claim → spot-check 5-10 citations; verify any direction-changing finding 100% before acting.
- Repeats prior sessions → instruct it to skip typical intro mapping; shift the time range; space sessions out.
- "Feels complete but unsure" → apply anti-demotion deliberately; ask what categories an expert would expect that you didn't request.
- Truncated/crashed output → raise the output-token cap; split into fewer areas per session; retry transient errors; reserve highest reasoning effort for already-audited prompts.

**Regime check.** Under-documented domains: the workflow ENABLES a map that was otherwise effectively impossible (alternative = expensive specialists). Over-documented domains: it only COMPRESSES time (the info is public, ~20-30h of manual reading). Calibrate value claims accordingly; the richest insights often hide in a Regime-A pocket inside a Regime-B domain.
