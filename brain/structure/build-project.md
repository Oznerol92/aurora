A reusable AI workspace ("project") has three parts that stop you re-explaining yourself every conversation:

- **Instructions** — the fixed _how_: role, scope/purpose, and method/voice rules. This is the system prompt; it holds across every chat. Cover role (peer-to-peer tone, no reflexive agreement or compliments), purpose (what kind of content, on what topic, done honestly and backed by numbers), how-we-work (verify against sources rather than memory; keep facts and inferences distinct; no sycophancy or dramatization; admit and fix errors without groveling), voice (the writing rules), and an explicit "what must never get out" list.
- **Knowledge** — the _what you know_: uploaded templates and a couple of strong worked examples to imitate.
- **Memory** — a small seed that grows with use: who I am and how I write, what I'm building and for whom, what must never reach public text, and where I've gotten to (last step done, next step). It keeps the thread between chats.

Every chat inherits the Instructions (method), sees the Knowledge (facts), uses the Memory (thread); the move is always orient-first, then narrow to the chat's goal.

**Public-method vs private-context separation.** The reusable mechanics — structure, voice, method rules, templates — are public and shareable. The identity-bearing context — real names, stack, accounts, the specific project — lives in the private "must never get out" layer and the memory seed, not in published output. The workspace sharpens over time only if tended: when something works, consolidate it into the Instructions; when something fails, record it as a mistake not to repeat.
