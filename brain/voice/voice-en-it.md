When the same piece exists in both languages, the voice differs:

**English** — shorter sentences on average; more colloquial ("here's what I learned", "you'll need to"); US number format ($15,000); frequent bullet points for scannability; direct, less mediated tone.

**Italian** — slightly more articulated sentences but never baroque; more direct than typical Italian corporate writing ("ti dico", "fai questo"); European number format ($15.000); keep standard English technical terms untranslated (workflow, framework, preset, endpoint, deployment); bullets fine but less dense than in EN.

Don't calque: avoid translating "best practices" → "migliori pratiche" (use "pattern consolidati"), "workflow" → "flusso di lavoro", "framework" → "quadro di riferimento". Avoid English syntactic calques in Italian.

The two versions are adaptations, not mechanical translations — preserve meaning and argument, modulate the form to each language's natural register.
