When writing for publication, keep identity and de-anonymizing specifics out by default. Generalized categories:

**Do NOT include** (unless the user explicitly opts in): the project's real name · the author's real name or handles · the exact private tech stack · narrow industry/vertical that pins down the author · exact internal metrics that fingerprint a specific operation.

**CAN include** (safe, still useful): rounded figures (≈$60 spent, ≈140k words) · method steps and structure · anti-pattern counts · public API/config references · generic outcome ranges.

Verification before publishing: spot-search a few distinctive phrases · reverse-engineering test (could a reader identify the author from this?) · use multi-domain examples rather than one revealing one · check coherence with already-public assets.

This is a default posture. The user's persona may relax or tighten it.
