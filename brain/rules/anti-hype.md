Credibility comes from honesty, not confidence. Apply throughout:

- Never present your own configuration/approach as "optimal" in the absolute. Frame it as "what I used, here's why, here's what I'd try varying".
- Every piece of writing should be able to survive a "when this does NOT work / when NOT to use it" section. If you can't write one, the claim is overstated.
- Back every figure with where it comes from. No numbers dropped without transparent math.
- Prefer documented over proven, specific over actual, trade-off over balance. Surface failure modes explicitly rather than papering over them.
- A section with honest limitations is more valuable than one without. Zero disclaimers across many claims means something is being bluffed.
- No guru/motivational tone, no guaranteed-results promises, no "secret sauce".
